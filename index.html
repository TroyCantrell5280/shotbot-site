Event day

How the day runs

Four stops, from the desk to close-out. If the computers stop, the signed card is still the record.

  1. 1

    Desk

    Walk-up check-in prints the scorecard. When the event uses classes, staff pick the class from that event’s list on the same check-in step. The code on the card identifies the shooter, so staff are not reading handwriting to know whose card it is.

  2. 2

    Paper card

    The card is signed, then photographed. Each target mark is read. The total is added from those marks by the software. A changed mark, the Ø, always goes to a person before it counts.

  3. 3

    Live results

    One board for the event. It shows class only when that event turned classes on. Shooters open a link with no account. The clubhouse TV shows the same feed. Each shooter also has a personal results page.

  4. 4

    Close-out

    The director locks the scores. People who opted in at check-in get a results text. Raffle draws look up the ticket. Each seller counts cash against the drawer.

On the day

What the day actually uses

These are the pieces an event runs on today.

  • Check-in prints the card

    Walk-ups are added at the desk. Registering prepares the scorecard for the printer, and a reprint keeps the same card.

  • Marks, then a total

    The photo is read target by target. The score is computed from those cells. Nothing types in a guessed total.

  • A person sees every change

    Cards the reader is unsure about, and any Ø, wait in a review queue with the card photo. A changed mark is not resolved on its own.

  • One live feed

    The public board, the clubhouse TV, and the personal page read the same results. The public page shows name, squad, score, and place, and class only when the event turned it on.

  • One live board, two doors

    Shooters watch the same results from a link with no account, or from the viewer app. A text goes out only after the director closes the event, and only to numbers that opted in.

  • Raffle, recorded

    Rolls are registered before tickets are sold. A sale records a serial range. The draw looks up who bought that serial.

  • Cash the same day

    Sales sit on a seller session with an opening float. At close, counted cash is compared with what the drawer should hold.

  • Awards you can print

    Places come from the scores. A tie is marked. The director records who takes the place, with a note, and can print the sheet.

  • Rules stay on the event

    A charity team shoot and a club shoot with classes are both setups. Classes, categories, and how a team total is added are settings, switched per event.

  • Files to take home

    Results, roster, cell marks, and sales can be exported. Filing scores is optional, per club.

Reliability

Built for the range

A dead phone does not stop the day

The event can run on an event-hub laptop at the clubhouse, with or without an outside line. Check-in and scoring stay on that laptop. A phone that dies, or a photo taken with no signal, does not stop the next shooter. Card photos wait on the device and upload once, with no duplicate, when the signal returns. A card that cannot be photographed can be entered mark by mark.

Paper is the official record

ShotBot transcribes the signed card. It does not replace it. If the system stops mid-event, the tournament finishes on paper. Every posted score keeps the card, the photo, and who reviewed it.

FAQ

Questions from the desk

Do shooters install an app?

They can. The app is a live results viewer only. The same board is also a link with no account.

What if a phone dies, or the range has no signal?

The event-hub laptop keeps check-in and scoring going. Card photos taken with no signal wait on that phone and upload once when it reconnects. The signed paper cards are still in the box if you need them.

Is the paper card still official?

Yes. The signed card is the record. ShotBot reads it. If the computers stop, you finish the tournament on paper.

Who decides a changed score?

A person. Any Ø on a card, whether a scorekeeper changed a mark or a protest did, goes to review. The reader does not pick a winner for that cell.

When do shooters see results?

The live board is up during the event, on a link and on the clubhouse TV. Personal pages use the same scores. Texts go out at close, only to opted-in numbers.

How do raffle tickets and cash get reconciled?

Each roll is registered, and each sale records a serial range on that roll. At the draw, the serial looks up the buyer. At close, counted cash is compared with the seller session.

Do we have to file scores?

No. NSCA filing is optional, per club. A club that files can export a results file. A club that does not file skips it.
